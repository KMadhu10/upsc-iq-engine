Indian Polity & Constitution|||Fundamental Rights (Article 12-35)|||UPSC GS Paper II: Part III of the Constitution, justiciable nature, reasonable restrictions parameters.|||State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951), Kesavananda Bharati (1973)|||Fundamental rights protect individual liberty against legislative tyranny. They are not absolute but qualified, meaning Parliament can impose reasonable restrictions on grounds of state sovereignty, public order, and morality.

Indian Polity & Constitution|||Presidential Ordinance Powers|||UPSC GS Paper II: Article 123, executive legislative actions, co-extensive boundaries constraints.|||D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar (1987), R.C. Cooper v. Union of India (1970)|||Article 123 grants the President power to promulgate ordinances during recess. An ordinance carries the exact same force as an Act, but it must be approved by Parliament within 6 weeks of reassembly. Re-promulgating ordinances without legislative review is a subversion of the democratic constitution.

Indian Polity & Constitution|||Directive Principles (DPSP)|||UPSC GS Paper II: Part IV, non-justiciable socialist targets, governance directives.|||Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980), State of Kerala v. N.M. Thomas (1976)|||DPSPs are non-justiciable fundamental guidelines for state policy mapping. The Supreme Court holds that the Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of a harmonious balance between fundamental rights (Part III) and directive principles (Part IV).
